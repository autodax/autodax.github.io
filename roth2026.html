Roth Conversion Planner 2026 tax year

Yearly checklist (about 5 minutes, each Oct–Nov)

The idea: convert a mild amount every year (the Auto plan), and convert more only if growth or your balance says RMDs are heading somewhere large. Your actual balance already reflects past gains and losses, but future spending and RMD estimates still depend on assumed growth. Update your balances each year, compare several growth assumptions, and adjust the plan rather than relying on a single forecast.

  1. Update the inputs. Set the start year to this year, enter your actual pre-tax (403(b), 457(b), IRA), Roth and brokerage balances, brokerage basis in dollars, and your year-to-date income. For a conservative spending read, set growth to about 5.5–6%.
  2. Set next year's spending. Read "Sustainable annual after-tax spending" (it keeps your age-95 cushion) and spend no more than that. Set "Plan at" to "Maximum sustainable spending" to run the whole table, conversions, RMDs and stress test at that spending level, so you see the actual drain-down path. Rerun every year: trim if returns disappoint, raise if they boom.
  3. Baseline conversion. Use this year's amount from the Conversion column in the year-by-year table. Re-estimate it from your actual income so the year's effective tax rate (federal income tax ÷ AGI) lands at the plan's cap.
  4. Check the triggers. Convert more, up to the top of the 24% bracket (taxable income of $201,775 in 2026, indexed each year), if any of these is true:
    • Your pre-tax balance (403(b) + 457(b) + IRA), in today's dollars, is above the safe line for your age ().
    • The stress test (growth +2 points) says your RMD would exceed the 22%-bracket limit (run the planner first at age 75, counting your Social Security).
    • At your normal growth setting, the row "first year the RMD exceeds that limit" shows a year instead of "never."
    If none is true, stay with the baseline. If less than about $10k of room is left, skip the year.
  5. Pay and time it. Finish the conversion before December 31 (your plan may cut off earlier). Pay the tax from brokerage cash. If you hold at least 110% of last year's total tax in withholding for 2026 (100% if your 2025 AGI was $150k or less), the tax is due by April 15 with no underpayment penalty.
  6. Stop at age 74. RMDs start at 75 for your birth year.

Safe line (largest pre-tax balance, today's dollars, that keeps the age-75 RMD in the 22% bracket)

  • Age 60: balance under $0.69M
  • Age 65: balance under $0.97M
  • Age 70: balance under $1.35M
  • Age 74: balance under $1.78M

It assumes the balance grows 7% a year above inflation with no withdrawals, so it errs toward converting. For the 24% bracket instead, multiply by about 2.2.

Watch-outs

  • Your plan must allow in-service distributions or in-plan Roth conversions while you work.
  • From about age 63, conversion income raises your Medicare IRMAA two years later.
  • Paying 32–35% to chase low future rates cost real spending in the tests. Stay at 24% or below.
  • Everything assumes today's tax law. This is general information, not tax advice.

Alternating shading helps track rows · bold = conversion year · green/gold text = RMD year. RMD = pre-tax (403(b) + 457(b) + IRA) start-of-year balance ÷ the IRS divisor for your age that year. Other pre-tax withdrawals cover living costs and taxes beyond the RMD (conversions are shown separately), so total withdrawals are the larger of the RMD or what you need. Taxable income is ordinary income (including taxable Social Security) after deductions. IRMAA: Medicare starts the November you turn 65 and each year's surcharge is based on AGI from two years earlier (2026 tiers and surcharges, inflated). It is deducted from your Social Security check once you claim, and billed directly before that, so it counts as a cash need. It is not a tax deduction.

Assumptions & method
  • Plan at chooses what spending the whole plan runs at. "My spending input" uses your after-tax spending figure. "Maximum sustainable spending" ignores that figure and runs the table, conversions, RMDs and stress test at the highest flat spending that never runs short and leaves your age-95 cushion. Each column is run at its own maximum, so compare the sustainable-spending row first; totals such as lifetime tax are then at different spending levels.
  • FICA is charged on the wages you enter: 6.2% Social Security up to the wage base ($184,500 in 2026, indexed at your inflation rate), 1.45% Medicare, plus 0.9% above $200,000. It is cash that goes out, is not income-tax deductible, and is not available to spend. Pretax 403(b) deferrals are not modeled, so enter gross wages.
  • Auto (default) tests 15 strategies: no conversions, effective-rate caps from 10% to 24%, and filling the 12%, 22% or 24% bracket, each through your last conversion age. It picks the one with the highest sustainable after-tax spending; among those within 0.2% of the best, it takes the lowest lifetime federal tax plus IRMAA. With the RMD check on, only strategies that keep RMDs inside the 22% bracket (even at growth +2 points) are considered. It uses the same engine as the wizard. The year-by-year table lists what to convert each year; it is a projection, so redo it each November with your actual income.
  • Fill a bracket every year, for life converts up to the top of the chosen bracket every year from the first conversion year through age 100, on top of any RMD.
  • Conversion modes: Convert fully by age (default) finds the smallest level yearly amount (start-year dollars, inflating) that empties the pre-tax accounts by the year you reach that age, with no bracket limit. Even out finds the smallest level annual amount (in start-year dollars, inflating) that brings the age-75 RMD to your ceiling, never taking taxable income above the chosen bracket and never converting outside the first/last year window. Fill modes convert as much as fits each year (front-loaded). An optional AGI cap for ages 63+ keeps conversions under an IRMAA tier two years ahead. Sustainable spending is the highest spending that never runs out and leaves the age-95 cushion.
  • Balances are entered as of Jan 1 of the start year, which is the first projected year. Growth and inflation compound once a year; results are shown in start-year dollars. Brackets, deductions and the age-65 extra deduction are 2026 IRS figures inflated at your inflation rate; Social Security thresholds and the $200k NIIT threshold do not inflate. The temporary senior bonus deduction (2025–2028) is included. Years after 2026 use the published 2027 figures, then inflate.
  • Living expenses and Social Security are entered in start-year dollars and inflate. Wages are taxable and entered before Roth contributions; Roth contributions come from after-tax cash. IRMAA starts the November you turn 65 and is based on AGI from two years earlier, so it is left out for any Medicare year whose income two years back is before your start year.
  • Each year, spending + Medicare premiums + Roth contributions + FICA + federal tax is paid from wages, Social Security and the RMD first, then cash above the amount you keep, then the brokerage (gains at the basis share, taxed at capital-gain rates), then the pre-tax accounts (403(b), 457(b), IRA, pooled), and the Roth last. The tax and the withdrawals that pay it are solved together, and every year balances to the dollar. The emergency fund is used only after a market drop, which a steady-growth projection never has; it still counts toward your assets and earns interest (taxed). The first Social Security year pays one month (November birthday).
  • Account by account (as in the wizard): each RMD comes from every pre-tax account in proportion to its balance; conversions and other pre-tax withdrawals come from the account chosen in "Convert and withdraw from first", then the smallest; each conversion goes to the matching Roth (403(b) to Roth 403(b), 457(b) to Roth 457(b), IRA to Roth IRA); Roth contributions from pay go half to the Roth 403(b) and half to the Roth 457(b); Roth withdrawals come from the smallest Roth first. All accounts grow at the same rate, so the split never changes the totals or the tax.
  • Dividends are qualified, reinvested and taxed each year; the growth rate is total return. RMDs use the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Same rules as the tax estimator. Medicare Part B premiums and IRMAA are paid on top of spending and are not deducted for tax. IRMAA uses AGI from two years earlier, so the planner skips it for any year whose AGI two years back is before your start year.
