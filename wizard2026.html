Step 3 of 5Roth conversion and how to pay the tax

A Roth conversion moves money from pre-tax to Roth. I pay income tax on it now, and it then grows tax-free, isn't counted toward future required distributions, and is never taxed again.

Which model should I use?
  • Auto with guardrails (my default). Tests 15 strategies against market history (or the simulated futures) and keeps the one that supports the most spending at my target chance; near-ties go to the lowest typical lifetime tax and IRMAA. The most realistic choice.
  • Auto. The same 15 strategies, judged with one steady growth rate. Faster and easier to follow year by year, but it ignores bad market stretches.
  • My tax target. Converts up to an effective rate, the top of a bracket, or a total tax amount that I choose. Good when I already know the rate I'm willing to pay.
  • A set amount. Converts exactly what I enter. Useful for trying "what if" amounts or matching a conversion already done.
  • Even out. Spreads conversions evenly so my first RMD at 75 stays under a ceiling I choose, without going past a bracket.
  • No conversion. Skips this year. The plan still shows what converting would do.
