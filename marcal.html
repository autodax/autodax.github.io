How federal and California interact

California starts from federal AGI, then subtracts all Social Security and takes its own (much smaller) standard deduction. It has no preferential capital-gain rate, no §199A QBI deduction, and no senior bonus deduction. Its brackets are separate, so a Roth conversion can be cheap federally yet land in California's 8–9.3% brackets. California tax is not deductible federally unless you itemize; this estimator assumes the standard deduction on both returns. The marginal rates above are measured numerically, so they include federal effects like extra Social Security becoming taxable and the senior deduction phasing out. Brackets are FTB's 2025 indexed schedules (latest published; 2026 will run roughly 2–3% higher). Exemption-credit phase-out for very high incomes is not modeled.

How this is calculated

The §199A deduction is the lesser of 20% of QBI and 20% of taxable income (before the QBI deduction) minus net capital gain. Above the taxable-income threshold, the 20%-of-QBI amount is also limited to the greater of 50% of W-2 wages or 25% of wages + 2.5% of UBIA, phased in over the range. Rental is not a specified service business. It reduces taxable income only, not AGI/MAGI, so it does not change Social Security taxability, NIIT, or the senior deduction phase-out. The $400 minimum deduction for materially participating owners is not modeled.
