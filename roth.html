IRMAA: Medicare starts the November you turn 65 and each year's surcharge is based on AGI from two years earlier (2026 tiers and surcharges, inflated). It is deducted from your Social Security check once you claim, and billed directly before that, so it counts as a cash need. It is not a tax deduction.

Assumptions & method
  • Tax ceiling = effective federal rate (federal income tax ÷ AGI, so it counts all your income: wages, taxable Social Security, withdrawals, dividends and gains). Conversion pattern: "Level" converts the same inflation-adjusted amount every year after your last working year (in working years, conversions fill only up to the ceiling rate); "Fill" converts each year until that year's effective rate reaches a fill rate. "Zero out" converts a level amount sized so the 403(b) reaches zero at 75, ignoring the tax ceiling (it still applies to working years). Otherwise the planner finds the smallest conversion level that keeps your effective rate at or below the ceiling in every year from age 75 on. IRMAA is not part of this rate.
  • Balances are entered as of Jan 1 of the start year, which is the first projected year. Growth and inflation compound once a year; results are shown in start-year dollars. Brackets, deductions and the age-65 extra deduction are 2026 IRS figures inflated at your inflation rate; Social Security thresholds and the $200k NIIT threshold do not inflate. The temporary senior bonus deduction (2025–2028) is not modeled.
  • Living expenses and Social Security are entered in start-year dollars and inflate. Wages are taxable and entered before Roth contributions; Roth contributions come from after-tax cash. IRMAA starts the November you turn 65 and is based on AGI from two years earlier, so it is left out for any Medicare year whose income two years back is before your start year.
  • Cash needs (living + Roth contributions + tax − wages − Social Security − RMD) come from the brokerage first (gains at the basis share, taxed at capital-gain rates). Once it is gone, living costs come from the 403(b) (tax is withheld out of the conversion), and from the Roth only if the 403(b) runs out.
  • Dividends are qualified, reinvested and taxed each year; the growth rate is total return. RMDs use the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Same rules as the tax estimator. IRMAA is paid from SS (or cash) and is not deducted for tax.
