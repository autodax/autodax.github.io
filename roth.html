IRMAA: Medicare starts Nov 2031 and each year's surcharge is based on AGI from two years earlier (2026 tiers and surcharges, inflated). It is deducted from your Social Security check once you claim, and billed directly before that, so it counts as a cash need. It is not a tax deduction.

Assumptions & method
  • Tax ceiling = effective federal rate (federal income tax ÷ AGI, so it counts all your income: wages, taxable Social Security, withdrawals, dividends and gains). Conversion pattern: "Level" converts the same inflation-adjusted amount every year from 2029 (2027–28, when you have wages, fill only up to the ceiling rate); "Fill" converts each year until that year's effective rate reaches a fill rate. Either way the planner finds the smallest conversion level that keeps your effective rate at or below the ceiling in every year from age 75 on. IRMAA is not part of this rate.
  • Balances are treated as the start of 2027. Growth and inflation compound once a year; results are then shown in today's (2026) dollars by dividing out inflation. Brackets, deductions and the age-65 extra deduction ($2,050 in 2026) rise with inflation; Social Security thresholds and the $200k NIIT threshold do not.
  • Social Security and living expenses are entered in today's dollars and inflate. Wages are taxable; Roth contributions come from after-tax cash.
  • Cash needs (living + Roth contributions + tax − wages − Social Security − RMD) come from the brokerage first (gains at the basis share, taxed at capital-gain rates). Once it is gone, living costs come from the 403(b) and the tax is withheld out of the conversion.
  • Dividends are qualified, reinvested and taxed each year; the growth rate is total return. RMDs use the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. 2026 IRS brackets, same rules as the tax estimator. IRMAA is paid from SS (or cash) and is not deducted for tax.
