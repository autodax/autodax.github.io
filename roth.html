Year by year (today's 2026 dollars)

Shaded = conversion year · bold = RMD year. RMD = 403(b) start-of-year balance ÷ the IRS divisor for your age that year. Other 403(b) withdrawals cover living costs and taxes beyond the RMD (conversions are shown separately), so total withdrawals are the larger of the RMD or what you need. Taxable income is ordinary income (including taxable Social Security) after deductions. IRMAA: Medicare starts the November you turn 65 and each year's surcharge is based on AGI from two years earlier (2026 tiers and surcharges, inflated). It is deducted from your Social Security check once you claim, and billed directly before that, so it counts as a cash need. It is not a tax deduction.

Assumptions & method
  • Conversion modes: Convert fully by age (default) finds the smallest level yearly amount (start-year dollars, inflating) that empties the 403(b) by the year you reach that age, with no bracket limit. Even out finds the smallest level annual amount (in start-year dollars, inflating) that brings the age-75 RMD to your ceiling, never taking taxable income above the chosen bracket and never converting outside the first/last year window. Fill modes convert as much as fits each year (front-loaded). An optional AGI cap for ages 63+ keeps conversions under an IRMAA tier two years ahead. Sustainable spending is the highest spending that never runs out and leaves the age-95 cushion.
  • Balances are entered as of Jan 1 of the start year, which is the first projected year. Growth and inflation compound once a year; results are shown in start-year dollars. Brackets, deductions and the age-65 extra deduction are 2026 IRS figures inflated at your inflation rate; Social Security thresholds and the $200k NIIT threshold do not inflate. The temporary senior bonus deduction (2025–2028) is not modeled.
  • Living expenses and Social Security are entered in start-year dollars and inflate. Wages are taxable and entered before Roth contributions; Roth contributions come from after-tax cash. IRMAA starts the November you turn 65 and is based on AGI from two years earlier, so it is left out for any Medicare year whose income two years back is before your start year.
  • Cash needs (living + Roth contributions + tax − wages − Social Security − RMD) come from the brokerage first (gains at the basis share, taxed at capital-gain rates). Once it is gone, living costs come from the 403(b) (tax is withheld out of the conversion), and from the Roth only if the 403(b) runs out. The planner does not minimize lifetime taxes. The first Social Security year pays one month (November birthday).
  • Dividends are qualified, reinvested and taxed each year; the growth rate is total return. RMDs use the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Same rules as the tax estimator. IRMAA is paid from SS (or cash) and is not deducted for tax.
